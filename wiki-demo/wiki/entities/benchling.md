# Benchling

The horizontal incumbent in lab-software for life sciences. ~800
employees, late-stage private (Series F, ~$6B valuation, last raise
2021). Started in DNA-design software, expanded into a broad lab-data
platform: notebooks, inventory, registry, workflows.

## Why they matter to [[Strand Bio]]

Benchling is the default answer to "what software does your wet lab
use?" in the protein-engineering segment Strand Bio is targeting.
Every founder Strand Bio talks to has either:

- already paid Benchling (and is annoyed about something specific), or
- evaluated Benchling and bounced off the per-seat cost, or
- has never heard of it because their lab is too small (Strand Bio's
  customers).

## Why Strand Bio isn't directly competing

Benchling is horizontal. Every wet-lab type, every team size, every
workflow. Strand Bio is vertical. Protein engineering, teams of 4 to
25, the specific bottleneck of wiring a liquid handler to a
notebook. The bet (articulated in [[2026-05-15 Building In Public Talk]])
is that horizontal incumbents always leave a vertical seam, and the
seam at this segment is *the orchestration layer between LIMS and the
hardware*.

The thing not to do: try to be a smaller, cheaper Benchling. That's
the failure mode the previous generation of lab-software startups
walked into. The right move is to be a different *shape* of product,
not a discounted clone.

## Where this shows up in operations

- Pricing model: Strand Bio sells per-instrument, not per-seat.
  Benchling's per-seat model is the friction point for small teams.
- Hiring filter: Avery actively does *not* hire engineers from
  Benchling. Cultural import risk is too high at 9 people. See
  [[Small Teams Compounding]].
- Sales motion: Strand Bio's first conversation with any prospect
  asks "what do you use today and what do you hate about it?". And
  the answer is almost always Benchling-shaped, which seeds the rest
  of the pitch.
