# 2026-05-20 Postpone Series A

Decided not to open a Series A round in Q3 2026. Push the raise to
mid-2027 (or skip it entirely if the company is default-alive on
revenue by then).

## Context

The seed round closed in late 2025 with 26 months of runway. Two
multistage funds had warmly indicated interest in leading a Series A
"whenever you're ready," with the implied expectation of Q3 2026.

[[Linh Park]] and [[Avery Chen]] disagreed cleanly: Linh wanted to
postpone, Avery initially leaned toward raising. The May 18 board
meeting was the forcing function.

## Decision

Don't raise. Re-evaluate after the inventory service hits 8 paying
customer labs (target: end of Q1 2027) and after the lab-orchestrator
core (the v2 product) is in alpha.

## Rationale

Three reasons, in the order they convinced Avery:

1. **The story is too clean to raise on cleanly.** A Series A
   raised now would be priced off three customer labs and a $0 ARR
   line; the narrative would be "team + thesis." Eighteen months
   later, the same round can be raised off real ARR and the
   v2 product in customers' hands. A fundamentally better story
   and a 2-3x better price.
2. **[[Linh Park]]'s reading was correct on capital intensity.**
   Strand Bio's burn does not scale linearly with team size at this
   stage. The bottleneck is product-market discovery, not
   compute, not legal, not GTM headcount. Adding $15M of dry powder
   doesn't compress that discovery curve; it expands the
   organizational radius to defend instead.
3. **[[Small Teams Compounding]].** The decision to raise is
   reversible (the funds will be there in 2027); the decision to
   grow past 14 people on premature capital is materially harder
   to reverse. The asymmetry favors waiting.

## What Avery had wanted

Avery had been pulled toward raising for two reasons that, on
inspection, did not survive:

- *"More runway is safer."* True for the company, false for the
  product. More runway with the same product velocity buys time
  to be wrong slower.
- *"The market window."* The investor sentiment for synth-bio is
  currently warm, and could cool. Acknowledged. And rejected as
  a reason. Optimizing for the market window is a story about
  the investors; the story should be about the product.

## Counterarguments considered

- *"What if the funds aren't there in 2027?"* The funds will be
  there for *somebody*. The question is whether they'll be there
  for us, and the only honest answer to that is "yes if we ship,
  no if we don't". Which is true at every stage.
- *"What about employee comp?"* Real concern. Mitigation: the seed
  round was raised with a deliberately conservative team-growth
  plan that the current burn rate is well under. Comp budget for
  the next two roles is already in the bank.

## Status

Board agreed unanimously on 2026-05-18. Two interested funds were
told privately on 2026-05-20 ("not now, target mid-2027, stay in
touch"). Both responded constructively; one offered a SAFE bridge
for the gap, which was declined. Reasoning for declining is in
[[Linh Park]]'s notes. The short version: bridges encourage drift,
and drift is the actual risk being managed against here.
