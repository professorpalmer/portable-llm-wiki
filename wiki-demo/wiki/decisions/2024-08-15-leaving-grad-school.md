# 2024-08-15 Leaving Grad School

Decided to leave the [[Hannah Wu]] lab at Stanford with a master's
degree instead of completing the PhD. The single most consequential
career decision in this wiki. Every later decision compounds from it.

## Context

Three years into a planned five-year PhD in computational biology.
Thesis was on active-learning methods for protein-structure
prediction. The work was going fine. Two first-author publications,
a third one in late drafts.

The trigger was reading a draft of a competing group's preprint that
matched what would have been thesis chapter four, six months before
[[Avery Chen]] would have submitted. The realistic completion timeline
suddenly stretched from 18 to 30 months.

## Decision

File master's-on-the-way-out paperwork. Take the standing offer from
a medtech startup [[Avery Chen]] had been quietly interviewing with.
Tell [[Hannah Wu]] in person two weeks before submitting the
paperwork.

## Rationale

Three reasons:

1. **The opportunity cost was now visible.** Before the competing
   preprint, the marginal year of grad school cost ~$60k in foregone
   industry salary. After the preprint, the cost was ~$60k *plus* the
   collapse of the thesis-as-distinct-contribution argument.
2. **The work that mattered was downstream.** Avery's interest had
   already shifted from "extend the method by 5%" to "deploy the
   method in a system where it actually changes a wet-lab decision."
   The lab couldn't be the place where the second sentence got
   answered. Industry could.
3. **The advisor's incentives diverged from mine.** [[Hannah Wu]] was
   compassionate but materially worse off if Avery left. One fewer
   senior student to push the lab's publication output during a
   review year. This is structural, not personal. It's also the
   first time [[Avery Chen]] internalized that not all
   well-intentioned mentors give advice optimized for the mentee.

## Counterarguments considered

- *"You're 80% done."* True for credit-hours; false for thesis.
- *"You'll regret not having the title."* Three years out, the
  evidence says no. The actual title in conversations is "founding
  engineer," and the PhD-on-LinkedIn would be a costume.
- *"The competing preprint might not pan out."* It did.

## What [[Hannah Wu]] said

In writing (recommendation letter, October 2024):
> "Avery is leaving the program in the way that I wish more of my
> students would. With a clear thesis they could no longer make,
> not with vague burnout. Their decision is correct under their
> stated objectives. I do not agree with every objective."

The last sentence is the unvarnished version. Avery saved it.
