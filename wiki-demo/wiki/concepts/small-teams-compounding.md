# Small Teams Compounding

A team of 9 that ships a thing every week compounds faster than a team
of 60 that ships every quarter. The advantage isn't speed in absolute
terms. It's that **a faster feedback loop produces better decisions**.
Better decisions, accumulated, compound the way interest does.

## The corollary

Hiring is risk management against the loop, not capacity expansion. Every
hire that *doesn't* fit the loop slows it down, regardless of how
talented they are. Two great people who can't ship together are a worse
outcome than one great person shipping alone.

This is why the [[Mia Patel]] hire took three weeks and why
[[Avery Chen]] sat in on every loop-test interview.

## Architecture follows team size

A 9-person team uses [[Boring Stack First]] not because the stack is the
answer, but because boring stacks don't require a person to babysit them.
Every minute spent on infrastructure is a minute *not* spent on the
product. The org chart is the architecture.

## When this breaks

It breaks at scale boundaries:

- 20 people: the daily feedback loop starts to need formalization
- 50 people: process becomes load-bearing
- 200 people: the strategy *is* the org chart

The principle holds, but the implementation differs at each tier. At 9
people the implementation is "everyone reports to the founders and we
demo every Friday." That doesn't scale to 200.
